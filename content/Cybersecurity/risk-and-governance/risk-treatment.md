Risk treatment connects an assessment of possible harm with a decision about what to do. That decision needs business context, the organization's tolerance for risk, and any applicable obligations. A simple likelihood and impact label is useful input, but it does not settle the treatment by itself.

Risk acceptance and risk transfer illustrate why the remaining exposure matters. Both can be appropriate in context, while leaving responsibilities and consequences that need attention.

## Conditional Risk Acceptance

A frequent event with a small individual impact may be suitable for acceptance with monitoring and proportionate safeguards. Acceptance is appropriate only if the residual risk falls within organizational tolerance and the decision is documented.

Residual risk is the exposure that remains after the safeguards in place are considered. Monitoring helps keep the acceptance decision connected to actual conditions, rather than treating it as permanent permission to ignore the risk.

High likelihood and low impact do not automatically justify acceptance. Repeated small losses may matter to the business. The processing context or legal obligations may also call for stronger treatment even when the impact of each event appears limited.

## Proportionate Safeguards

Using proportionate controls means matching the response to the risk and its context. A description such as low impact does not establish that minimal safeguards are sufficient for every organization.

The acceptance decision therefore needs to account for the controls already applied, the remaining exposure, and the conditions under which that exposure is tolerable. Monitoring gives the organization a basis for reconsidering the decision when those conditions change.

## Insurance and Risk Transfer

Cyber insurance transfers some financial consequences of incidents to an insurer. This is a form of risk transfer. The extent of that transfer depends on the policy.

Limits and exclusions leave residual risk with the organization. Insurance also leaves the organization responsible for its security. A financial arrangement cannot by itself establish that the underlying systems or information are protected.

## Limits and Tradeoffs

Acceptance requires a justified decision within risk tolerance; it cannot be inferred from likelihood and impact labels alone. Insurance transfers defined financial consequences without eliminating incidents or all resulting losses. Review both decisions in terms of the exposure that remains.
