Organizations need to understand what information they hold and how it should be handled. Data classification connects sensitivity to protection. A privacy impact assessment examines risks arising from the processing of personal information. These concepts support related decisions, but each has its own purpose.

The practical starting point is the information and its intended use. A sensitivity label and a new analytics capability both need to be considered in terms of their consequences for handling data.

## Classification as a Handling Policy

A data classification policy defines how information should be protected according to sensitivity. It connects categories of information with rules for access, storage, sharing, and disposal.

For example, confidential customer records need tighter handling than public brochures. This comparison explains why classification matters: applying identical handling to every kind of information would ignore meaningful differences in sensitivity.

The label is only a starting point. Its meaning must carry through to the relevant handling rules. Marking a record confidential does not itself restrict access or change how it is shared.

## Assessing New Personal Data Processing

A Privacy Impact Assessment examines privacy risks before introducing new processing of personally identifiable information (PII). PII includes information such as names or activity linked to individuals.

Launching a new analytics platform that processes this information is a strong reason to assess privacy risks before the processing begins. The assessment brings attention to the effect of using personal information in the proposed context.

Classification helps identify the sensitivity of information involved. Privacy assessment addresses the processing and its risks. A handling label alone does not replace that assessment.

## Limits and Tradeoffs

Classification labels need enforced handling rules. Review what the category requires in practice, rather than assuming the label itself protects the data.

Whether a formal privacy assessment is legally mandatory depends on jurisdiction, processing context, and risk. Not every activity involving PII automatically triggers a formal Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA).
