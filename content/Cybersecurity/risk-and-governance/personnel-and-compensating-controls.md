Security controls include policies and staff procedures as well as technical safeguards. Personnel controls create opportunities for oversight. Compensating controls provide comparable protection when an intended safeguard cannot be implemented. Both require attention to how the control works in practice.

Their value depends on the risk addressed. A procedure needs a clear purpose, rather than being treated as protective simply because it exists.

## Mandatory Vacations and Job Rotation

Mandatory leave and job rotation can reduce insider and fraud risk, particularly when misconduct depends on one employee continuously controlling a process. Another person takes over and may discover hidden actions or unusual records.

In an illustrative Security Operations Center (SOC), a replacement analyst reviews alerts closed during a colleague's earlier shifts. Repeated suspicious dismissals become visible during that review. The control creates an opportunity to discover the pattern; the handover alone does not guarantee discovery.

Rotation also reduces dependence on a single person. Meaningful handovers and independent review make the transfer of responsibility useful for both continuity and oversight.

## Distinguishing Separation of Duties

Separation of duties divides sensitive steps among different people. Vacations and rotation can support oversight, but they do not create that division by themselves.

This distinction helps keep the stated protection accurate. Having someone else cover a role occasionally differs from assigning separate people to sensitive parts of a process.

## Compensating for an Infeasible Safeguard

A compensating control is an alternative safeguard that provides comparable protection when the intended control is infeasible. The important relationship is between the original protection needed and the protection provided by the alternative.

A compensating control may be temporary, but duration is not its defining feature. Calling an arrangement temporary does not establish that it addresses the original risk adequately.

## Limits and Tradeoffs

Personnel controls increase opportunities to detect abuse without guaranteeing detection. Their effectiveness depends on independent review and meaningful handovers.
